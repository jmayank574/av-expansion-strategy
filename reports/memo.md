# Where should the operator launch after Las Vegas?

*For the strategy team. Data as of 2026-11-06. Sources: Census ACS 2024 5-year, NHTSA SGO ADS reports, NOAA GHCN-Daily, EIA, FAA, and researched seed files (AV rules, competitors) as of 2026-11-06.*

**Recommendation.** Enter Miami, Los Angeles, Phoenix and San Francisco first, then Houston, Tampa, Orlando and Atlanta; hold New York and Chicago until the rules change. Wave 1 is not one launch date: Miami and Phoenix launch first; Los Angeles and San Francisco (California) start the permit process now, with paid service only after permits. San Francisco only just clears the 50% gate (54%). The sequence uses balanced-weight scores for 25 US metros (demand 35, operating conditions 25, regulation 20, competition 15, charging 5). Waves 1 and 2 are limited to states with Tier 2+ AV rules; wave 1 also needs a top-5 finish in at least half of 10,000 weight draws.

**Why these metros** (rank, score, demand score, regulation, competitors, confidence; fleet at 3% capture of principal-city ride-hail trips, not the whole metro, so a metro can rank high with a small fleet)

- **Miami** (#1, 63.2): demand 78, Tier 3 (fast new-entrant path), Tesla and Waymo operating; top 5 in 97% of draws; ~56 vehicles.
- **Los Angeles** (#2, 60.9): demand 63, Tier 3 (slow new-entrant path), Waymo operating; top 5 in 91% of draws; ~263 vehicles.
- **Phoenix** (#3, 58.2): demand 38, Tier 3 (fast new-entrant path), Waymo operating; top 5 in 65% of draws; ~79 vehicles.
- **San Francisco** (#5, 57.7): demand 76, Tier 3 (slow new-entrant path), Zoox and Waymo operating; top 5 in 54% of draws; ~115 vehicles. Left of the ease median (48 vs 55) because of a crowded field (crowding 2.5; Zoox and Waymo operating) and a slow new-entrant path in CA (Tier 3).
- **Wave 2:** Houston, Tampa, Orlando and Atlanta (top 5 in 27%, 33%, 12%, 9% of draws). Demand 34-61 (wave 1: 38-78). Watch: weather is the main shortfall for all four (rain ~64-77 days a year).
- **Wave 3:** New York and Chicago. Demand 82-92, but driverless commercial service is not yet allowed. Build the policy case now; launch when the law changes.
- **Dallas-Fort Worth and Houston.** Dallas-Fort Worth ranks #18 of 25 (49.4): Tier 3 rules with a fast new-entrant path, held back by middling demand (51/100) and the most crowded field of 25 (crowding 3.0; Avride, Tesla and Waymo operating). It is in no wave. Houston ranks #6 of 25 (56.2): Tier 3 rules with a fast new-entrant path, held back by middling demand (53/100) and weather (rain ~64, snow ~0, 40 C+ ~1 days a year). It is in wave 2.
- **Why Phoenix is #3:** points versus the median metro come from operating conditions (+6.7), regulation (+4.7), power price (+0.4); demand (-4.6), competition (-1.5) pull it down. Its rank rests mostly on operating conditions, not on strength across the board.

**Deprioritize.** Dallas-Fort Worth (crowding 3.0); Sacramento (demand 20/100); Minneapolis (snow ~24 days, Tier 1 rules); Seattle (mid-pack on every pillar); Charlotte (demand 22/100); Austin (demand 18/100, crowding 2.5); Nashville (demand 12/100); Pittsburgh (snow ~22 days). None ranks better than #11 in any scenario (Dallas-Fort Worth).

**Key risks and de-risking**

- *Regulation:* 2 of the top 10 sit in Tier 0-1 states; engage IL and NY regulators early. CA is Tier 2+ but needs multi-step permits for a new entrant.
- *Weather:* No launch metro has meaningful snow; Miami has rain on ~92 days and Phoenix ~89 days at 40 C or higher. Validate heat and wet-weather performance before public launch.
- *Competition:* Miami, Los Angeles, Phoenix and San Francisco already have operators (crowding 1.3-2.5); Phoenix is the least crowded launch metro (1.3). Competitor status is dated and cross-checked against NHTSA activity.

**Confidence.** Miami is top 5 in 97% of weight draws and San Francisco in 54%; Atlanta is the softest pick (9%), so wave 2 order is not firm. A low-risk lens puts Phoenix, Miami, Tampa, Los Angeles and Orlando on top; fast growth, Miami, New York, San Francisco, Los Angeles and Chicago. Las Vegas, the beachhead, scores "Opportunistic" on this model (demand 36/100), which shows it underweights tourism-driven demand: visitors are not in resident population, density or commute data. The answer would also change if a Tier 1 state legalized driverless service, a competitor entered Phoenix, or fare and capture differ from our labeled assumptions.

**Data.** ACS 2024 5-year (2020-2024); FAA CY2025 enplanements; NOAA GHCN-Daily airport-station weather 2016-2025; NHTSA SGO reports to Sep 2026 (activity only, never rates); EIA August 2026 YTD; AV rules, new-entrant paths and competitors researched 2026-11-06 (some rows medium/low confidence); demand is a two-anchor estimate (Chicago and NYC ride-hail trips), not a forecast.
